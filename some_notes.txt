For your model, these address two different constraints:
\[
\text{waiting-time estimate}\approx
\frac{\text{number of misses}\times\text{latency}}{\text{MLP}}
\]
\[
\text{bandwidth-time estimate}\approx
\frac{\text{actual DRAM bytes}}{\text{bandwidth}}
\]
Even with substantial overlap, transferring extra bytes consumes bandwidth.

However, your concern about accounting for the same effect twice is valid. Both calibration experiments run with prefetching enabled. Consequently, the measured MLP ratio already reflects whatever prefetching and bandwidth effects occurred in that experiment; it is not a perfectly isolated hardware constant.

The model should therefore treat MLP and B_eff as related empirical measurements, and validate their combination. Dividing measured access time by MLP again, or adding a prefetch penalty already included in that measured time, could double-count effects.

The first-level TLB is misdetected. hw.json says l1_entries: 1024 with a 13.6 ns miss cost. The real first-level TLB is about 96 entries with a ~1.8 ns miss cost: the step at 128 pages.
Cause: the probe's line placement i*512 + ((i*7)%64)*8 repeats every 64 pages, so it uses only 64 of the 2048 L2 sets.
At 1024 pages that is 16 lines per set in a 10-way L2. The extra +11 ns is L2 conflict misses, not TLB.
The 2048-entry second level and the 101 ns walk come from the counter and are fine. On 256 MB tables almost every random access walks anyway, so the impact is small, but random accesses over 4–8 MB spans are over-charged.

Think about further such tradeoffs while calculating the parameters and what effect they can have ?
